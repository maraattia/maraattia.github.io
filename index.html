Dr. Mara Attia (they/them)

Exoplanet Scientist

SNSF Postdoc.Mobility Fellow, Kapteyn Astronomical Institute, University of Groningen

I study how planets between Earth and Neptune in size change over billions of years. Their orbits can migrate, their atmospheres escape to space, and their molten interiors trade gas with the atmosphere above. I build open-source models of these processes and use them to reconstruct the histories of planets observed today.

I did my PhD at the University of Geneva and moved to Groningen in 2025 on my own fellowship from the Swiss National Science Foundation, to work at the Kapteyn Astronomical Institute alongside Tim Lichtenberg's Forming Worlds Lab. Beyond my host group, I co-lead the Dutch node of a Cambridge–Toronto–Groningen collaboration on open model ensembles, abiotic baselines for interpreting exoplanet observations.

Research

Interiors

What a planet is made of, and whether its mantle is molten or solid, sets its radius and how much water and gas it can store. I wrote PALEOS to compute this.

Atmospheric escape

Starlight strips gas from close-in planets, and light species leave first. I work out how escape changes both the mass and the composition of an atmosphere.

Orbital histories

Many close-in planets did not form where we see them. I model how their orbits migrated and what the journey did to their atmospheres.

Open-source codes

PALEOS

Equations of state and mass–radius relations for iron, silicate, and water at planetary-interior conditions.

JADE

Coupled orbital dynamics and atmospheric escape of planets in three-body systems.

ZEPHYRUS

Atmospheric escape for planetary evolution models, which I am rebuilding around six escape regimes.
